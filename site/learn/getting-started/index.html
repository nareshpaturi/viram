Getting started with pranayama

Begin with one gentle technique for a few minutes, sitting comfortably and breathing softly. Viram’s beginner program, Pranayama Foundations, starts with Sama Vritti (box breathing) for 3 minutes, then adds one technique at a time: extended exhale, then ocean breath, then alternate nostril breathing, then humming bee breath.

Written by Viram from the app’s own guides. Updated October 10, 2026. How we write our guides.

The beginner path

Learn five classical techniques one at a time, then join them. Each new technique plays its introduction first. These are its single-technique sessions, in order:

  1. Session 1 · 3 min Box breathing Sama Vritti In 4 · Hold 4 · Out 4 · Rest 4
  2. Session 2 · 5 min Extended exhale Visama Vritti In 4 · Out 6
  3. Session 3 · 5 min Ocean breath Ujjayi In 5 · Out 5
  4. Session 4 · 5 min Alternate nostril breathing Nadi Shodhana In 4 · Out 6, each side
  5. Session 5 · 5 min Humming bee breath Bhramari In 4 · Hum 8

Your first practice in Viram

  1. Choose. Begin the practice ready on Breathe, pick a technique from Practices, or start Pranayama Foundations under Programs.
  2. Settle. The first time you practise a technique, a short spoken introduction explains it. Then three quiet seconds to settle, with the technique’s care note.
  3. Follow the guide. A voice, soft tones, or haptics mark each step, with the on-screen guide. Pause anytime; ending asks first, and the practice is saved to History.

How long, and how often?

Any length from 1 to 60 minutes, or a set number of rounds. Most guides start at five minutes; 4-7-8 breathing is taught in rounds and starts at four. Pranayama Foundations moves on one session at a time, and Viram can remind you once a day if you’d like.

Practising safely

  • Stop and breathe normally if you feel dizzy, tingly, short of breath, or anxious.
  • Chest pain, fainting, or a pounding heart with dizziness: stop and get medical help.
  • Don’t practise while driving or operating machinery.
  • Never practise breath holds in or near water, including the bath.
  • Living with a heart or lung condition, or pregnant? Check with your clinician first, especially before holds.
  • If focusing on your breath makes you more anxious, stop. That happens to some people and is normal.
  • Breathing practice is not a treatment and doesn’t replace medical care.
