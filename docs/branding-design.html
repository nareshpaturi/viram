01 / Position

Make room for a real practice.

For people who practice pranayama, and people curious to start, Viram is a free, offline companion: faithful technique guides, a ready-to-start practice, and timing you can make your own.

One promise

Authentic pranayama, on your terms.

Lead with faithful teaching and ease. Guides explain how, why, and when to take care. Custom timing and sharing support the practice. Local history quietly remembers it.

Lead line: Steady breath. Steady mind.
Descriptor: Pranayama, guided at your pace.
Secondary line: Your breath. Your rhythm. (rhythm adjustment and saved rhythms)
Name gloss: Viram (विराम): a pause, a place to rest.
01
Welcoming

One clear next action. Familiar words. Safety and cue choices are easy to understand.

02
Faithful

Teach each technique as it is practiced: its name, steps, care notes, and sources. Never claim authority the product hasn't earned.

03
Personal

Begin with a gentle default; adjust it when you want. Nobody has to build a rhythm to start.

04
Steady

Predictable timing, quiet surfaces, and permission to pause. The product never evaluates how well someone breathed.

Primary

The practitioner

Yoga practitioners, teacher-training students, and teachers who want authentic techniques without a subscription.

Secondary

Follows a guide with gentle defaults. Never has to understand ratios to begin.

Distribution

The teacher who shares

Sends this week's practice to students as a link. The link carries the rhythm; Viram supplies the guidance.

The lead line echoes Hatha Yoga Pradipika 2.2, which ties the steadiness of the mind to the steadiness of the breath. Present it as a line from the tradition and never pair it with outcome claims. Retired: “Breathe by your numbers” (it implied a biometric product), the v2 descriptor “Guided breathing, at your pace” (it didn't name the practice), and “Your breath. Your rhythm.” as the lead line (it spoke to settings, not the practice). See the brand exploration for the options considered.

02 / Identity

One name. One familiar mark.

Viram everywhere: in the app, in conversation, and in stores as “Viram: Pranayama & Breath.” Viram (विराम) means a pause, a place to rest; in Hindi, purna viram is the full stop. Say “vee-RAHM.” The mark's coral point is that pause between breaths.

Viram

Two breaths

Two equal arcs make one open circle: inhale and exhale, left and right. The coral point rests in the gap at the top: the pause between breaths, the viram that gives the product its name. The open bottom is the rest. Use this construction everywhere the brand appears: app icon, welcome, lockups, share card, web page, notifications. The mark stays still during practice; the circular guide carries the motion.

Small-size specimens

Keep clear space of at least one quarter of the mark width. Minimum size: 16 px with the pine field, 24 px without it. Keep the store source square and opaque; the platform supplies its icon mask. Android adaptive icons keep the mark inside the 66% safe zone.

  • Grid100 × 100; circle of radius 28 centered at (50, 52), set slightly low so the coral point balances the weight.
  • ArcsStroke 9, round caps; 28° gaps at top and bottom measured from the center. The gaps never close.
  • Coral pointRadius 4.6, centered in the top gap; the only coral in the mark.
  • ColorLeft arc sky, right arc mist, point coral, field pine. One-color versions use pine or paper throughout.
  • Assetsassets/brand-*.svg are the sources; npm run brand:assets renders the app icon, adaptive and monochrome icons, favicon, and splash from the same construction.

The right arc is drawn darker here for visibility on mist; in production it is mist on pine.

Don't rotate, stretch, close the gaps, fill the circle, color both arcs alike, add glow, or substitute a leaf, lotus, Om, or other symbol. Replaced on September 22, 2026: the drop-and-cross mark, which read as a leaf and as a double-barred cross. Name and cultural-language clearance, plus a trademark search on the mark, remain prelaunch tasks.

Naming practices

Romanized name first, English name second: Nadi Shodhana · Alternate nostril breathing. Use the common romanization people search for, without diacritics. Every guide adds a pronunciation respelling and a pronunciation clip; the approved clip is the reference, not the respelling.

The bundled fonts include macrons and ś but not the underdot and overdot letters of full IAST, so the UI doesn't use them. Adding full IAST requires a covering font, such as Noto Serif. Hindi localization uses Devanagari with its own font.

āīūśḍṣṭṇṃ

Dashed: not in the bundled font files; these fall back to a system font here.

No new colors. Techniques reuse the four step colors by kind: sky inhale, saffron hold, coral exhale and hum, mist rest. Sides and sounds are expressed by shape, label, and voice, never by color alone.

Soft Light

Everyday screens sit in soft light made only from the brand colors: a few wide radial glows over a pale base, never a hard band or a new hue. Added October 1, 2026; it replaces the earlier rule of no gradients on brand surfaces.

Washes follow the day

Tab screens take the wash for the local hour, decided when the screen opens. No setting is needed. Reduce Transparency keeps the wash but makes cards solid.

Frosted cards

Cards are translucent white with a white rim and a soft pine shadow, no outline. Text on them keeps paper contrast.

Rhythm orb

Each practice's circle is lit by its own rhythm: sky, saffron, coral, and white glows sized by the seconds in each step. Sides add a split; hum adds ripples. Decorative; numbers always accompany it.

Practice glow

During practice, the current step's color glows softly into the pine around the guide, and the disc is lit from the top left. Night practice dims both.

Line diagrams

Technique diagrams use one 1.8 px pine line, paper and mist fills, and plain labels. Mirror view; no faces, bodies, or decoration. The one exception is the Welcome aura: a single-line nose at the center of a four-color aura, because pranayama begins with the breath through the nose.

Icons

24 px grid, 1.7 px rounded strokes, no fills. The Breathe tab uses a wave in a circle, echoing the mark.

Motion

One gentle ease that follows the breath, with no overshoot. Screens change with a short fade, never a slide or zoom.

Coral point

Appears once per composition: in the mark, at the head of a progress arc, or on a selected day. Never decorative.

No stock yoga posesNo lotus, mandala, or OmNo sunsets or oceansNo hard or off-palette gradientsNo photography in v1

One spoken voice

Calm, warm, and unhurried. Practitioners choose one of six voices (American, British, or Indian English), and that one voice speaks every cue, introduction, and pronunciation clip. No whispering or performance. Optional music (a tanpura drone or a soft pad) sits well under the voice, in the tones' key, and never competes with it.

Short cues, same words

Cue words match the on-screen labels exactly and last at most 0.8 s; side phrases such as “Inhale left” at most 1.2 s. Introductions stay under about 40 seconds and always have captions.

Honest production

AI-generated once under a commercial licence and bundled. Sanskrit names use a pronunciation dictionary and are approved by a Sanskrit or Hindi speaker. About discloses the AI voice.

Exact step vocabulary: Inhale → Hold after inhale → Exhale → Rest, plus Hum, left, and right for techniques that need them. Explain Rest as the pause after exhaling. Use “round,” not “cycle.” Keep “Begin,” “Pause,” “Resume,” “End session,” “Done,” and “Breathe again” consistent. Teach each technique faithfully; never claim lineage authority or an instructor review that hasn't happened.
06 / Launch kit

A promise the first release can keep.

Show the v1.0 experience in store imagery: the library, a guide, practice with voice cues, and sharing. Routines, reminders, and Health stay out of launch copy until v1.1 ships. Wearable charts and streaks never appear.
